# Cash flow basics for a new small business

This is a short general guide written for this project. Replace it with your own material over time.

## Profit is not the same as cash

A business can be profitable on paper and still run out of money. Profit counts a sale when you make it; cash flow counts money when it actually arrives or leaves. If customers pay you 30 days late while you pay suppliers immediately, you can be short of cash even during a good month.

## Build a simple 12-month cash forecast

1. List your expected cash coming in each month: sales you expect to be paid for, loans, or owner contributions.
2. List your cash going out each month: rent, supplies, wages, insurance, loan payments, and taxes you will need to remit.
3. Subtract outflows from inflows to get the net change, and carry the closing balance into the next month.
4. Look for the first month the balance goes negative. That is when you need a buffer, a credit line, or a change in plan.

## Practical ways to protect cash

- Invoice promptly and state payment terms clearly, for example "due within 15 days".
- Ask for a deposit on larger jobs or custom orders.
- Keep an emergency buffer covering two to three months of fixed costs where possible.
- Set money aside for sales tax and payroll remittances as you collect it, because it is not yours to spend.
- Review the forecast monthly and compare it to what really happened.

## Pricing and break-even

Your break-even point is the sales volume where revenue equals total costs. Divide your fixed monthly costs by the profit you make on each sale (price minus the variable cost of that sale) to see how many sales you need each month before you make money.
